# Shape library — 2026-09-19

A library run of the mask-first method (`docs/METHOD.md`, "At scale") over
**282 everyday-icon concepts** in eight categories (`data/library-concepts.json`):
nature, animals, vehicles, places, UI symbols, sports, food and objects. Eight
drawing workers (one per category) drew masks, gated them with the tether-aware
structural screen and exact shipped-roll threading, and — new for this run — a
blind judge who never saw the concept list named every threadable mask before any
fold was spent. A second drawing round used the judge's actual labels as feedback.
Only judge-named variants were folded (≤2 per concept), with a 240 s and a 400 s
retry pass for violators.

The seven shipped demo shapes, the 37 winners of `docs/EXPLORATION-20260919.md`
and the harvest-era rejects were out of scope; `house`, `fish`, `star`, `key`,
`smiley`, `question-mark` and `music-note` (rejected under the old Chamfer method,
never drawn mask-first) were drawn fresh.

## How to read the table

- **verdict** as in `docs/EXPLORATION-20260919.md`: `PASS`, `violating (n)`.
  Only judge-named concepts were folded, so every row here was recognizable to a
  blind judge before it cost fold time; rows that are `violating` after the
  400 s pass are budget statements about the fold, not about the mask.
- **variant** is the judge-preferred passing variant (`explore_summary.py
  --prefer`), not necessarily the mechanics-best one.
- **finish**: `flat` or `STANDING`, tracked by replaying the move sides.
- Recognizability remains a human gate (`docs/RULES.md`). The blind judge is a
  prefilter; the final pick is made from the numbered blind sheets
  `docs/library-20260919/pick-blind-0[1-4].png` (labeled keys next to them,
  `pick-key.json`). Both passing variants of a concept appear on the sheets when
  both passed.

## What the library run taught (beyond the exploration rules)

All eight workers and both judge rounds converged on the same findings, each
confirmed by exhaustive walks (`tools/design_search.py --mode walk`):

- **Exactly symmetric silhouettes almost never thread.** Symmetric caps,
  teardrops, arches, discs, domes-on-stems, tiered trees and centred stems fail
  parity or are roll-UNSAT; the winner is the one-cell-off-centre neighbour.
  Only 4 of 19,175 centred row-profile 27-cell shapes thread.
- **A drawing gets exactly two 1-wide tips** (the chain ends). Rays, legs,
  prongs, spikes, antlers, tentacles, binder rings, crossing bars and arrowheads
  are all third tips: sun, snowflake, spider, octopus, gear, target, clock,
  peace, yin-yang, recycle, hashtag, dollar, ampersand, X, female, download,
  crown spikes, chess rook/knight, kite, skis, medal, fence, ladder, glasses,
  scissors and skateboard are structurally out at 27 cells.
- **A 1-wide stalk must land on the end cell of a wider block**, never its
  middle (periscope, antenna, wick, flame, spire, chimney, tent pole): after the
  chain leaves the stalk one side of the block is a dead end.
- **The widest row's end cells are tips** unless something sits above or below
  them, so lens hulls, cigar bodies, saucers and rounded-bottom envelopes are
  roll-dead; wheels work as 2-cell corner bumps under a full-width row.
- **Two-wide lobes next to a 1-wide tip** (comb beside a beak, fluke beside a
  spout) are a same-side double turn at joints 2–3 and always UNSAT.
- **The blind judge names far fewer masks than their authors.** Workers rated
  ~150 concepts as reading; the judge named 57 after round 1 and 81 after the
  feedback round. Silhouette families collapse: "block + thin bar" (hammer /
  key / flag / lollipop), "wide blob with a hole" (car / camera / toaster / dog),
  "two rings + bar" (glasses / binoculars / bicycle), "tall stem + small head"
  (candle / lighthouse / tower). Landmarks that survived at 27 cells: a
  periscope, a raised tail, a handle ring, a door notch, wheel holes, a spout,
  a giraffe neck, a rabbit's ears, a snake's tongue.
- **Fold side.** 59 % of judge-named masks passed at 150 s, 38 % of the rest at
  240 s, 14 % of the rest at 400 s; the remaining violators are table incursions
  under a machine that was shared with other search campaigns the whole run
  (load average 30–160 on 8 cores), so their budgets were effectively smaller.
- **Tooling.** `cubot.viz.render_silhouette` projects onto the two longest
  axes and transposes tall drawings; the first judge pass was invalid for that
  reason and `tools/blind_judge.py` now renders tiles straight from the rows.

## Per-category notes (condensed from the workers' reports)

**Nature.** Rounded, symmetric organic forms are the roll's worst case: sun
rays, snowflake arms and cherry stems are extra tips; crescents never satisfy
parity with both horns on the majority colour; rain streaks must sit on the
cloud's outer column. Named blind and passing: crescent-moon, mountain,
mushroom, rainbow; cloud and tornado fold only with violations. The round-2
carrot (bushy cap wider than the body), one-armed saguaro, waisted pear,
stem-and-leaf apple and lollipop tree thread and their authors rate them as
reading, but the blind judge did not name them.

**Animals.** The hardest category. Two 1-cell tips at block corners (cat ears,
claws, wing tips) are UNSAT across ~540 k completions while 2-tall tips on a
full-width row thread reliably; two-lobed flukes are UNSAT on every stalk;
symmetric faces (pig, crab, butterfly) fail until nudged. Named blind and
passing: bear, cat, dinosaur, dog, duck, fish, frog, giraffe, rabbit, snake
(with a tongue; giraffe and dinosaur are 1-wide necks with a head); bird and
owl fold only with violations.

**Vehicles.** Hand silhouettes with two 1-cell wheel tips were UNSAT for every
vehicle; wheels as 2-cell corner bumps or ring holes under a full-width row are
the working vocabulary. Pointed noses on 3-wide bodies are UNSAT. Named blind
and passing: bicycle and motorcycle (two rings + frame), bus, car, helicopter,
rocket (stepped nose, notched base), train, truck (semi with a gap between cab
and trailer); airplane and tank fold only with violations.

**Places.** A symmetric pitched roof with a door never threads; crenellations,
pickets, rungs, eaves and sails are tips or rings. Named blind and passing:
arch, bridge, castle, door, house (flat ridge + eave + door), pyramid (offset
ziggurat), road-sign, stairs, window; lighthouse folds only with violations.

**UI.** Filled blobs (solid triangles, rounded squares) almost never thread —
serpentine U-turns land on forbidden joints — so nearly every winner is a 1-wide
outline with one gap or one spur; a ring carries at most one spur. Named
blind and passing: battery, ghost, home, key, lock, magnifier, phone,
question-mark, robot, smiley, speech-bubble, trash; music-note (beamed pair)
folds only with violations.

**Sports.** Symmetric odd-width profiles (pawn, pin, trophy, hoop, medal) fail
parity; handles on a trophy are impossible; chess pieces lose their tops.
Named blind and passing: golf-flag, hockey-stick, soccer-goal (posts taller
than the bar), tennis-racket; chess-pawn folds only with violations.

**Food.** Wide-over-narrow stacks (burger, cupcake, hotdog, pizza wedge) thread
only as lopsided blobs, and the lopsided blob is what the judge misreads.
Named blind and passing: bottle (1-wide neck on a 3-wide body with one
chamfered shoulder), cake (candle on two tiers), donut (ring with a bite), fork
(two unequal tines), knife, teapot, wine-glass; ice-cream and spoon fold only
with violations.

**Objects.** Handle + head reads when the handle is 1- or 2-wide and the head is
3-wide and off-centre. Named blind and passing: camera, candle (with a drip),
chair, crown, headphones, laptop, magnet, pencil (eraser cap), shoe, table, tv,
wrench; broom, hammer, shovel and sword fold only with violations.
